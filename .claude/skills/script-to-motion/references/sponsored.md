# Sponsored videos — working from a brief

A paid campaign comes with a brief: required lines, required on-screen proof, forbidden claims, an upload checklist. The
payout depends on every item. This file is how the Kling 4.0 × Higgsfield campaign video met its brief, and the
judgement calls the user made along the way.

## Contents
1. Read the brief into a requirement table
2. The product is the star — it's an ad
3. Required lines and proof

5. Prices
6. Checking compliance

## 1. Read the brief into a requirement table

- Separate **hard requirements** ("must meet all requirements to be approved and paid") from **suggestions** ("tip",
  "suggested flow", "works well"). Quote the brief's own words in the table: requirement → where it is met (cue id,
  chapter, file) → status.
- The brief is usually **confidential** ("for your personal use only"): never on screen, never shared.
- Keep the table in the progress file and re-check it after every content change.

## 2. The product is the star — it's an ad

- Open with the product's best finished output, played whole.
- Leave out side incidents that aren't about the product (another tool's safety filter, retries of an image model, the
  user's setup trouble). Trial and error only where it concerns the product's outputs, and briefly.
- **No "can't" statements or negative framing.** "할 수 없는거에 대해서는 굳이 언급할 필요없어. 이거 광고야." A limitation
  the brief doesn't ask you to mention stays out; one the brief asks for (an honest verdict) is phrased as what to watch
  for ("이어지는 장면을 만들 때는 자리 배치를 꼭 확인하세요"), and the verdict ends on the strength.
- Grade generously where it's a judgement call ("참 잘했어요" stamp, not a middling score) — while the facts stay true.
- Brag about what the user values in the product when it's true and sourced: "품질도 좋으면서 같은 비용으로 훨씬 여러 번"
  with the sponsor's own comparison on screen, plus a short personal remark in the user's voice.
- No CTA for anything but the product (the user removed "다음 이야기를 댓글로" — "이거는 힉스필드 클링 광고잖아").
  A light closing gag that asks for comments about the video itself is fine.
- Don't build a premise that demeans a real occupation (the user rejected a delivery-worker plot); keep the conflict
  about appearance or situation.

## 3. Required lines and proof

- Say every required sentence on camera, close to the brief's wording. It may span two cues ("…최대 30초까지 만들 수 있어요.
  / Kling 3.0의 15초보다 딱 두 배죠.") — check the pair, not one cue.
- Show the required workflow on the product's real website with generations happening on screen; at least the number of
  modes asked (all of them if feasible).
- Put the plan next to the result for every mode; play finished outputs in full (references/footage.md).
- Use the official media kit where it adds proof (official samples, official comparisons), with provenance entries.
- Chapters as questions with the product/feature name.

## 4. Claims, competitors and IP

- Forbidden promises (e.g. 4K, HDR, longer extensions not yet available): search narration and on-screen text for them.
- Only claim what the interface shows ("don't promise any limit or option you can't see on screen").
- **Third-party brand names**: don't name competitors in your own narration or copy. A comparison the sponsor made
  (their media kit) can be shown as it is, labels included; the product's own UI showing other models' names is the
  product, not third-party IP (the user's decision). If a line names a competitor because the user asked for it, flag
  the risk once when asked about compliance and offer the neutral wording.
- No third-party logos, characters, footage, real titles or scenes; generated people only; reference images the user's own.
- Data in a trend section: keep sources in `research.md`. The user removed on-screen source chips; mark estimates as
  estimates in the line itself ("추정되는데요").

## 5. Prices

"생성요금쪽은 실수하면 안되는 부분이거든." Pricing is where an ad gets the facts wrong most easily.
- Use **only numbers the product's own button showed in the recordings** — crop the frame and read it. Briefs and pricing
  pages can disagree with the button and with each other; the button wins, and you never quote the brief's figures
  against it.
- Always qualify: **"촬영 시점(날짜) 기준 · 언제든 바뀔 수 있음"** — once in narration, on every on-screen price, and in
  any text that carries a number.
- **No period-bound wording** ("출시 할인 기간", "until …", "지금만"): the video is watched for months.
- No judgement that depends on today's price ("부담 없이") unless it is the sponsor's own sourced comparison.
- Account-specific discounts are "제 계정 가격"; show the list price beside it if the button shows both.

## 6. Checking compliance

- When asked "가이드 충족하는지 확인", go through the table item by item and fix what's missing. Answer exactly the scope
  asked.
- "100% 충족해?" gets per-item facts and the judgement calls named (a competitor named on request, a translated anchor
  text), not a blanket yes.
